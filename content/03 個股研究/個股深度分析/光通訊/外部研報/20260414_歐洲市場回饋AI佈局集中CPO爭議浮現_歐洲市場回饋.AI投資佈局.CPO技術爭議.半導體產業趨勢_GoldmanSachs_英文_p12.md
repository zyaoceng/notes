Taiwan Technology: Europe marketing feedback: AI positioning remains 
concentrated; CPO debates emerging
13 April 2026 | 1:42PM CST
We met with over 35 investors during our recent marketing trip in Europe. Overall, we 
continue to observe strong interest in AI-related opportunities, with investors largely 
focused on structural growth names across the semiconductor value chain. However, 
we sense that positioning has become increasingly selective, with capital 
concentrated in a narrow group of high-growth names, while valuation sensitivity has 
started to emerge following the recent rally. Against this backdrop, valuation has 
become a key topic of discussion, with investors questioning what constitutes a 
reasonable level at this stage of the cycle. Our view remains that fund flows are likely 
to stay concentrated in high-growth AI names, reinforcing the valuation premium for 
structural winners. 
Our top stock highlights this round include TSMC (Buy on CL), GPTC/All Ring (Buy; 
beneficiaries of advanced packaging upcycle), Aspeed (Buy; AI/general server 
strength), Hon Precision (Buy; testing beneficiary). Below, we summarize the key 
takeaways from our meetings. 
CPO as one of the key debates among investors 
CPO continues to be a highly debated topic among investors, although visibility on 
adoption remains limited at this stage. Discussions primarily focused on the timing of 
adoption, end customer readiness, and the infrastructure changes required to 
support CPO deployment. Skepticism remains elevated, with investors questioning 
the end customer adoption of CPO, and highlighted that Broadcom has been 
promoting the technology for several years without seeing meaningful large-scale 
adoption so far.  
TSMC: positioning, weighting and competition remain key focus 
TSMC continues to be a core focus among investors, and it continues to be one of the 
key core holdings among European investors with some long-only investors already 
holding close to 10% of TSMC of their portfolios. Key discussions about TSMC during 
our meetings centered on both operational and positioning aspects. Investors raised 
questions around the phase-out of mature nodes and how this could translate into 
advanced packaging capacity expansion, particularly given cleanroom constraints. 
While there are also concerns around potential MSCI weighting adjustments, given 
TSMC’s significant index weight.

competitors could catch up over the next five years. Our view remains that this is

MediaTek: near-term uncertainty vs long-term ASIC optionality 
MediaTek saw more mixed sentiment among investors. While some remain constructive

smartphone outlook (which is largely hampered by the rising memory pricing) remains

also raised the possibility of shifting ecosystem dynamics, particularly if AWS were to 
strengthen relative to Google. 
We think potentially a more attractive entry point could emerge closer to mid-2026, we 
believe the market would largely price in the smartphone weakness by then and start to 
focus on its ASIC opportunity into 2027 as the company approaches the mass

revenue ramp in 2027 and beyond.

ASE: viewed as TSMC proxy with mixed perception on value proposition 
Investor views on ASE remain somewhat polarized. Some investors question its role as a

effectively serves as a proxy for TSMC’s packaging roadmap and will continue to benefit

Aspeed: strong conviction with a straightforward growth narrative  
Aspeed remains one of the most favored names among European investors, supported 
by its clear and straightforward growth narrative as well as its unique market 
positioning. Investors like the BMC market competition landscape with basically only 
two players in the market and Aspeed as the largest player with 70%+ of market share. 
In general, European investors are constructive on the company’s BMC TAM expansion, 
which is being driven by the transition firstly from general server to AI server, and now 
further into Agentic AI, alongside ongoing ASP uplift with the increasing penetration of 
its new AST2700 BMC. While some concerns remain centered on its high valuation, 
however, we highlighted that Aspeed has been trading at an average of 40x in the past 
10 years, we believe that as long as the competitive landscape and the BMC TAM growth 
outlook remains favourbale to Aspeed, Aspeed would continue to trade at this high 
valuation.

Hon Precision: multiple ASP drivers and competitive positioning in focus 
Investor discussions on Hon Precision were primarily focused on its multiple drivers of 
ASP expansion, including contributions from AOI (automated optical inspection) which 
could lift ASP by around 10-15%, future technologies such as MCL (microchannel lid), 
ongoing handler upgrades, and potential upside from CPO both in terms of pricing and 
volume. Another key discussion point was its competitive positioning versus Chroma, 
with investors seeking clarity on long-term market share dynamics. Our view remains 
that the market could gradually evolve toward a more balanced structure, potentially 
approaching a 50/50 share split within AI/HPC over time under the backdrop of 
customer’s dual source strategy. 
See also: Strength from AI/HPC demand continues with CPO opportunities rising as potential 
new growth engine; reiterate Buy; TP up to NT$5,700 (13 March 2026) 
All Ring / GPTC: clear beneficiaries of advanced packaging upcycle 
Investor sentiment toward All Ring and GPTC remains constructive, with limited 
pushback on the core investment thesis. Both names are viewed as key beneficiaries of 
TSMC’s capex upcycle and the broader advanced packaging technology roadmap.  
In particular, as we highlighted in our report - Entering the next structural upcycle beyond 
CoWoS; reiterate Buy; TP up to NT$3,500 (24 March 2026), GPTC’s investment case 
continues to center on dollar content growth, driven by the ramp-up of SoIC (System-on 
Integrated Chips) and panel-level packaging, as well as recurring revenue from chemical 
consumption, which supports its premium valuation. 
As for All Ring, we highlighted its potential earnings upside from CPO opportunity in 
2027, along with solid momentum from advanced packaging expansion trend, we 
believe that as the industry migrates from CoWoS to panel level solutions going forward, 
this will likely spark another capacity addition upcycle. See also: CPO opportunity emerging 
as a game changer to drive the next earnings upcycle; upgrade to Buy from Sell; TP up to 
NT$800 (11 March 2026)

Investment Thesis, Price Target Risks and Methodology 
Investment Thesis - TSMC (2330.TW/TSM) 
TSMC is a leading global foundry company specializing in cutting-edge nodes. Its leading 
technology stance enables it to enjoy more than 60% of revenue share in the global 
foundry market. We like TSMC as we believe its solid technology leadership and 
execution better position it vs. peers to capture the industry’s long-term structural 
growth opportunities, particularly in areas such as AI/5G/HPC/EV. In addition, valuation 
looks attractive in our view, with the shares trading at the mid-range of their 10-year 
trading history (P/E: 10-29x). Furthermore, we view TSMC as the key AI enabler among 
our Taiwan Semis coverage, thanks to its leadership stance in leading edge nodes and 
advanced packaging technology - CoWoS (chip on wafer on substrate). We believe TSMC 
will achieve its 25% revenue CAGR target for the next several years, driven primarily by 
increasing silicon content growth and AI/HPC demand, with LT GM to remain at 56%+. 
We are Buy rated. 
Price Target Risks and Methodology - TSMC (2330.TW/TSM) 
Valuation methodology: We have a 12m TP of NT$2,750, which is derived by applying a 
target P/E multiple of 22x to our 2027E EPS. Our 22x target P/E is benchmarked against 
1.0stdv above its 5-year trading average. For the ADR (TSM), we have a 12m TP of 
US$550, based on a USD/TWD rate of 30.0 and an ADR premium of 20%. 
Key downside risks to our views: (1) further deterioration in end-demand recovery 
impacting capacity utilization; (2) slower customer node migrations; (3) slowdown in AI 
investment resulting in lower long-term semiconductor content growth; (4) poor 
yields/execution resulting in worse-than-expected profitability; (5) stronger 
competition resulting in ASP/profitability erosion; and (6) unfavorable FX trend or 
higher-than-expected cost increase weighing on the margin outlook. 
Investment Thesis - Aspeed (5274.TWO) 
Aspeed is a leading Taiwan-based IC (integrated circuit) design company specializing in 
BMC (baseboard management controller) SoC, which is used to remotely monitor the 
physical state of servers. The company is the largest BMC supplier, with c.70% of the 
global BMC market share, and 90% of its total revenue comes from the BMC segment. 
We like Aspeed as we expect its revenue/earnings CAGRs to further accelerate to 
52%/60% in 2025-2028E (vs. 5.9%/4.9% during 2019-2023), driven mostly by AI server 
demand, especially started from 2025. Furthermore, we also expect server architecture 
changes will bring more business opportunities to Aspeed to drive up its TAM expansion 
within the server market. With more TAM expansion and more dollar content growth 
within the AI theme, we continue to see upside potential to its revenue and earnings 
growth. We have a Buy rating on the name.

Investment Thesis - Hon Precision (7769.TW) 
Hon Precision is a leading Taiwan-based semiconductor testing equipment provider 
specializing in test handler and Active Thermal Control (ATC) system, which are widely 
adopted in Final Test (FT) and System-Level Test (SLT). Hon Precision enjoys >90% of 
market share in the FT handler market for AI/HPC applications, enabling it to fully 
capture growth in both AI GPU and AI ASICs.  
We like Hon Precision as we expect its revenue/earnings to accelerate in coming years 
driven by 1) growth in AI GPU/ASIC volume, 2) larger chip package size, 3) longer testing 
time, 4) higher power and thermal requirements, 5) rising adoption of SLT among AI 
ASICs, and 6) shorter chip upgrade cadence. Hon Precision shares are trading at a much 
lower valuation than its Taiwan/global peers in terms of forward P/E. We view the 
company as one of the most attractive names within our TW Advanced Semi Testing 
coverage, with meaningful re-rating potential as the market begins to reflect its strong 
earnings growth outlook.  
Price Target Risks and Methodology - Hon Precision (7769.TW) 
Valuation: We are Buy rated on Hon Precision with a 12m TP of NT$5,700, which is

analysis) to our 2027E EPS.  
Key downside risks to our views: (1) softer AI/HPC demand, (2) slower adoption of SLT 
among AI ASICs, and (3) intensifying competition.  
Investment Thesis - GPTC (3131.TWO) 
GPTC is one of the premier suppliers of semiconductor wet processing equipment, 
primarily utilized in back-end advanced packaging processes. The company 
manufactures semiconductor wet processing equipment, including metal etching 
equipment and single-wafer cleaning equipment, as well as providing engineering and 
installation services to semiconductor manufacturers. GPTC is the main CoWoS wet 
clean equipment provider and has 50% of market share at TSMC and near 100% of 
market share at ASE/SPIL. GPTC is also the sole provider for SoIC wet clean equipment at 
TSMC given its leading technology versus local peers.  
We favor GPTC as we expect its revenue/earnings CAGR to further accelerate to 
28%/41% in 2025-28E, driven by 1) continued advanced packaging capacity expansion, 
and 2) equipment ASP increase amid rising advanced packaging (e.g. SoIC, CPO, FOPLP) 
technology complexity. The company is now trading below its AI cycle (2023-2025) 
average forward P/E of 29x; however, with proliferation of advanced packaging into 
non-AI application and increasing ASP thanks to rising technology complexity, we 
continue to see upside potential to its revenue and earnings growth and maintain a Buy 
rating on the name.

Investment Thesis - All Ring (6187.TWO) 
All Ring is one of the key suppliers of semiconductor equipment in Taiwan, primarily 
utilized in back-end advanced packaging processes. The company manufactures 
underfill dispenser, TIM heat sink attach equipment, and automated optical inspection 
equipment which are widely adopted within the CoWoS process. All Ring currently has 
nearly 100% of market share in WoS underfill dispenser and TIM heatsink attach 
equipment, and its main customers include leading foundries and OSATs in Taiwan. 
We like All Ring as we expect its revenue/earnings CAGR to further accelerate to 
42%/47% in 2025-28E, driven by 1) continued advanced packaging capacity expansion, 
and 2) rapid growth of its CPO business. The company is now trading below its AI cycle 
(2023-2025) average forward P/E of 22x; however, with All Ring’s unique position in CPO 
coupling equipment and new packaging opportunity such as PLP, we see upside 
potential in its revenue and earnings growth and have a Buy rating on the name.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

MORE NOISE ON CHINA OPTICAL MODULES NAMES
Reuters reported over the weekend that U.S. Senator Dave McCormick introduced the “Securing National Security Systems from 
Chinese Optical Transceivers Act” (S.5548). This is not an FCC update; it’s proposed legislation that has to move through the full 
process - pass the Senate, clear the House, and be signed by the President - before it becomes law. Sharing snippets from 
McCromick’s press release below, and the current can be tracked on congress.gov under S.5548.
The Securing National Security Systems from Chinese Optical Transceivers Act would close that gap by extending safeguards 
Congress has already established for Chinese semiconductors to optical transceivers used in national security systems. The 
legislation would:
•
The Securing National Security Systems from Chinese Optical Transceivers Act would close that gap by extending safeguards 
Congress has already established for Chinese semiconductors to optical transceivers used in national security systems. The 
legislation would:
•
Prohibit federal procurement of covered optical transceivers for national security systems, building on the existing federal 
framework restricting certain Chinese semiconductors.
•
Cover InnoLight, Eoptolink, and their subsidiaries and affiliates, as well as additional adversary-linked companies designated 
by the Secretary of War or Secretary of Commerce, including covered transceivers that incorporate their firmware, software, 
or components.
•
Provide a five-year transition period before the prohibition takes effect, allowing trusted American and allied manufacturers 
time to expand production while minimizing disruptions to federal supply chains.
•
Direct the Department of Commerce to assess U.S. and allied production capacity and develop a strategy to strengthen trusted 
optical transceiver supply chains.
•
Permit limited, renewable waivers when no trusted alternative is available, with each waiver reported to Congress.

Japan Tech Analysts’ HK marketing feedback: the tone is constructive, but investors are still searching for the next catalyst 
to re-gross -  macro noise remains high and there’s no single, clean investment narrative yet. On the components side, Akinori 
Kanemoto flagged a long bias toward MLCCs, with investors watching Japanese vendors to follow through on the announced 
price hikes. Quality still screens as long Murata for most, but some prefer Taiyo Yuden on valuation upside; more interesting 
to Kanemoto-San, both LO and HF have gotten meaningfully more constructive on TDK as the classic Long Murata/Short 
TDK pair trade shifts toward being long both. He also noted additional investor discussions across Rohm, aluminum 
electrolytic capacitors, Kyocera, Niterra, and Nidec. 
On semis and tech materials, Mio’s takeaway was similar: fundamentals are broadly viewed as strong, but valuation discipline 
is tighter than usual. Most conversations clustered around three items: 1) Advantest’s SoC tester outlook, 2) whether front-end 
equipment players can push through price increases, and 3) Kioxia catalysts (shareholder returns and SLC). Her long-term 
preference stack is Advantest and Tokyo Electron at the top, followed by Kioxia Holdings and JX Advanced Metals; near-to-
mid term she prefers SCREEN Holdings.

Microsoft copilot launch puts A lot more focus on enterprise end-use than consumer. Samik Chatterjee views the 
announcement as Microsoft's attempt to establish Copilot as the front door to productivity and AI tool leverage for knowledge 
workers, and as a clear articulation of the Seat + Consumption model, on account of several factors, including: 1) the $30 user 
subscription license remains the anchor for Chat and Office in Copilot, while Cowork, Code, Autopilot and frontier models 
(Astra, Fable) move to usage-based billing via Copilot Credits; 2) new FinOps controls across Agent 365 that give IT the 
spending policies, model restrictions, and user-level caps that should limit the friction of variable pricing; 3) the decision to 
consolidate the consumer and commercial apps and step back from the personal companion market; and 4) press reports 
earlier this week of seat discounts for 1,000+/10,000+ seat customers tied to usage commitments, which Samik view as 
trading near-term ARPU for broader deployment ahead of the usage layer.

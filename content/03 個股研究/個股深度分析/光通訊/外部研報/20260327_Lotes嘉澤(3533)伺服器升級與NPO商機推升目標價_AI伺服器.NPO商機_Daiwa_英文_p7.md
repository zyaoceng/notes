What’s new: We expect Lotes to be one of the key beneficiaries of the 
regular server upcycle due to increasing inference demand. 
 
What’s the impact: Rising demand from regular servers for inference 
tasks. As we highlighted in our sector flash (23 March), we believe: 1) 
Agentic AI is driving higher inference demand than before; and 2) CPU 
servers can handle some inference tasks (likely the low-end part), while 
high-end inference will still be conducted by ASIC AI servers, considering 
the total cost per token. Thus, the market expects regular server shipments 
to grow by 30% YoY globally in 2026 with potential upside. However, Lotes’ 
management argues that “random shortage” will constrain the total regular 
server shipments globally in 2026 (Lotes targets growth of 15-20% YoY). If 
we look at the bigger picture for the next 2-3 years, we believe regular 
server demand will further be driven by rising inference requirements. As 
such, we expect the unmet demand will likely be pushed out to 2027. In this 
note, we adjust our growth assumption to c.20% and 16% YoY for Lotes’ 
regular server shipments in 2026E and 2027E, respectively. 
 
New growth opportunities. During its 4Q25 results call, Lotes unveiled its 
target to achieve c.20% of total sales from new growth opportunities in 
2026 (including QD, SOCAMM, NPO sockets, inner busbars, LGA 
connectors, high speed cables/connectors, etc.). NPO sockets are among 
the hottest topics for investors currently. We expect c.30m units of optical 
engines to be used within Nvidia’s scale-up and scale-out switches in 2026, 
with each using one NPO socket at an ASP of USD10-15. Considering 
Lotes’ 30% market share globally, this NPO story should bring an additional 
c.6% of total revenue contribution for the company in 2027, on our 
estimates. Although we are not as bullish as other investors on the NPO 
contribution, we expect this to be a good catalyst for Lotes to further re-rate 
on valuation grounds. In the near term, we see several tailwinds coming 
together to boost sentiment on the stock.   
 
What we recommend: We lift our 2026-27E EPS by 2-9% to factor in our 
higher regular server shipment assumptions. We also introduce our 2028 
EPS in this note. We reaffirm our Buy (1) rating and raise our 12M TP to 
TWD3,000 (from TWD2,000), based on a higher PER of 26x (from 18x; the 
high end of its past-5-year range of 10-28x) applied to our 1-year forward 
EPS, to factor in the new growth opportunities. Our 2025-28E EPS CAGR 
of 31% implies a PEG of 0.84x at our TP, which we consider undemanding. 
Key downside risk: lower-than-expected adoption of server CPU platforms. 
 
How we differ: Our 2026-28E EPS are 3-28% higher than the consensus, 
likely due to our more aggressive regular server shipment forecasts.

Company profile 
Founded in 1986 and listed on the Taiwan Stock Exchange in 2007, Lotes is one of the world’s leading 
connector vendors specialising in CPU socket/ILM and connectors, such as DDR, PCIe, SATA, and 
Type-C, with most of its production capacities at its Guangzhou, Zhongshan and Suzhou plants. Its

Lotes declared a DPS of TWD41.5 in 2025, representing a payout ratio of 50%. Being a leading
connector vendor for applications such as desktops, notebooks and servers, Lotes grew rapidly
along with its key clients (Intel and AMD) on strong technical capabilities and higher growth rate
from the server market. The solid revenue CAGR in the past decade required higher working
capital and continuous capex to expand its capacity, which could also be the culprit for its slightly
lower payout ratio compared with other electronic component makers. Having said that, we think
Lotes’ higher revenue growth should partially offset the slightly lower payout ratio.

Lotes constructed the RBA system in December 2010 and requires its suppliers to meet the
standards of no child labour, no forced labour, treat employees with respect and dignity, and
care for its labourers' safety and health. Lotes hosted a yearly supplier conference to promote
the RBA guidelines to its suppliers from 2013. During the same period, Lotes also asked its
suppliers to sign the RBA Code of Conduct. For better supply chain management, Lotes will
conduct a yearly audit and regular business review and hold the yearly supplier conference as
measures. Lotes compile the "Social Responsibility Commitment Letter" in 2021, to extend social
responsibility concept and requirements to the supply chain.
For “green” purchase management, Lotes passed the IECQ QC 08000 in 2008. Lotes will ask its
suppliers to follow the RoHS 2.0, HSF, and REACH guidelines to avoid using hazardous
materials. In 2023, Lotes completed conflict minerals due diligence for 29 direct suppliers,
achieving a 100% response rate. Overall, Lotes believes it has constructed a comprehensive
system from system certification (eg, ISO 9001, etc), product design, supply chain management,
and production techniques to ensure that the entire manufacturing process from raw materials to
end products are all related to green concepts. All productds from Lotes are now 100% under
RoHS standards.
